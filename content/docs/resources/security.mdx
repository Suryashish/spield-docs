Spield is engineered to be safe by construction, but **no DeFi protocol is risk-free**. This
page lays out the real risks plainly, and what Spield does to reduce each. Read it before
committing funds.

<Callout type="warn" title="The golden rule">
  Only deposit what you can afford to lose. Spield is young, unaudited by any published independent
  report, and its launch caps are small on purpose. Start small, understand each product, and verify
  solvency yourself.
</Callout>

## Smart-contract risk

Like any on-chain protocol, Spield runs on code. A bug could in principle affect funds. No independent
audit report has been published.

**Mitigations.** The yield engine asserts solvency after every state change. Contracts follow
checks-effects-interactions, amounts are bounded, and each contract exposes its live code hash so you can
verify what is running. Upgrades are **timelocked**: the delay is at least 24 hours (and at most 30
days), giving users a window to exit before code under their funds changes.

**Your part.** Use the official app and the verified addresses in [Addresses](/docs/developers/addresses),
and keep amounts small.

## Yield-source risk: Blend

On Blend series, Spield supplies USDC to a Blend lending pool and inherits that market's health.

**What it means.** If Blend works normally, Spield works as designed. If Blend were to pause withdrawals or
suffer bad debt, redemptions would wait on it, and in a severe case value could be lost. If the pool
loses principal, the loss lands **pro rata** across SR holders (nobody exits ahead of anyone else), and the
Fixed-Rate Vault applies a transparent pro-rata haircut to open receipts rather than freezing them.

**Liquidity in the pool.** Blend only lets USDC out up to its available liquidity. When the pool is
short, an unwrap or redemption can pay in parts. The app shows **Withdraw what is available** or
**Collect rest**, and you collect the remainder later.

**Mitigations.** Spield only **supplies** USDC (it never borrows), SR redemption never depends on a maturity
or on the rate being up, and the solvency view stays readable even if the pool were frozen.

## USTRY risks (tokenized Treasuries, testnet today)

USTRY series carry their own risks, on top of the contract risk above.

- **NAV can fall.** PT is measured in dollar face but pays out a USTRY amount fixed at the high-water NAV.
  If NAV falls, that USTRY is worth fewer dollars. USD face is **not** a principal guarantee.
- **Issuer controls.** The USTRY issuer's asset flags include **revocable** authorization and **clawback**. The
  issuer can freeze or claw back USTRY. A clawback from a series' contract freezes USTRY payouts for every
  user of that series until the tokens are restored. A clawback from a PT/USTRY pool makes trading fail
  closed, and LPs then exit pro rata from what the pool still holds.
- **Oracle risk.** USTRY series read a **SEP-40** NAV feed (RedStone on mainnet, a Spield-run feed on
  testnet). A stale or unavailable feed closes **new deposits and splits only**. Exits, claims, YT transfers, and
  burns never call the oracle, and finalization after the grace period does not read it either. The
  high-water NAV can only rise by a capped amount per year, so a bad price cannot move value faster than that.
- **Cash-out liquidity.** Converting USTRY to USDC uses the Stellar DEX order book. Spield does not redeem
  USTRY for fiat on your behalf, and a thin book means a worse price.

## Market risk (PT before maturity)

A PT held **to maturity** returns par (1.0). But its **market price before maturity** moves with interest
rates, like any bond.

**What it means.** If you sell PT early when rates have risen, you may receive less than you paid. Held to
maturity, you get the full fixed return.

**Mitigations.** None needed for hold-to-maturity savers. If you might exit early, size accordingly and watch
the market price.

## YT-specific risk

YT is the **higher-risk** leg. It is a leveraged claim on yield and can **decay to zero** by maturity if the
position earns little.

**What it means.** If realized yield underperforms the implied rate, YT can lose most or all of its value.
That is the trade-off for its upside leverage. There is no borrowing, so there is nothing to liquidate.

**Mitigations.** This is inherent to the instrument. Only go [long yield](/docs/guides/long-yield) if you
understand and accept it. You can claim accrued yield along the way and exit any time before maturity.

## Liquidity and slippage risk

Trades on the market are subject to available liquidity and slippage.

**What it means.** Large trades on a thin pool get worse prices, and a trade can exceed what the pool can
fill. LP positions are exposed to the pool's price moving while they are in it.

**Mitigations.** Every trade shows a live quote and respects a **max slippage** you set (default 1%). If
the pool cannot fill your size, the app says so instead of sending a bad trade. LPs can always withdraw, so
liquidity is not trapped.

## Admin and governance risk

Spield's contracts are administered by a **2-of-3 multisig** account:
`GDJ66WMVVH6MWBL4KORY2HOXWLY4UGFCMP64RC474LUJPQCPQUC4RUFL`. It has three signers of equal weight, its master
key has weight 0, and every threshold is 2, so no single signer can act alone. It is the admin of all
mainnet contracts.

**What the admin can do.** Pause *new* deposits and trades, set the per-series deposit cap, adjust the
vault rate and fees **within hard on-chain ceilings** (vault rate 20%, yield fee 10%, treasury swap-fee share
50%), and schedule **timelocked** upgrades (24 hours minimum).

**What the admin cannot do.** Move user funds, mint unbacked tokens, drain reserves, or skip the upgrade
timelock. Those functions do not exist. A pause can never trap funds: PT redemption, vault receipt
redemption, YT yield claims, liquidity removal, and SR unwrapping stay open.

**What remains.** Two of the three signers acting together are trusted not to ship a malicious upgrade
during a window you do not notice. The timelock exists so that you can notice. Admin changes use two-step
rotation. See [Architecture: trust model](/docs/developers/architecture#trust-model-honest-summary).

## Per-series caps (Guarded Launch Cap)

Each series has a ceiling on total deposits. Mainnet Blend Series 1 was capped at 50 USDC, Series 2 at 160 USDC, and the live Series 3 at 50 USDC. The live USTRY series cap the backing behind PT and YT (36 USTRY on Series 1, 300 on Series 2) and unsplit receipts (10 and 50 USTRY).
Caps limit how much can be exposed to a bug at launch. They gate new deposits
only: lowering a cap, or a series being full, never blocks an exit.

## Immutable expiry

A series' maturity is fixed at creation. There is no roll and no extension. A new maturity is a new series
with its own contracts and its own PT. That limits what an admin can change after you deposit, and it
means you must move to a new series yourself when one ends.

## Cross-chain risk (only if you use it)

The app's cross-chain flows use **Circle CCTP V2**, which is separate infrastructure with its own risk profile.
They only move USDC to or from your own Stellar wallet. If anything pauses, the funds stop in your wallet.
The app re-reads the chain before every step and never repeats one, and a price more than 2% worse than you
reviewed stops a journey for your decision. If you would rather avoid it, withdraw USDC directly on
Stellar from an exchange.

## Wallet and phishing risk

Anyone can issue an asset with a similar name. PT assets are identified by their full **`CODE:ISSUER`**, and
each series has a different issuer. Verify against [Addresses](/docs/developers/addresses). Never enter your
recovery phrase into a website.

## How to stay safe

- Verify you are on the **official app URL** (`app.spield.live`).
- **Review** every transaction before signing. The app shows what each route does and how many signatures it needs.
- Check the **[Solvency dashboard](/docs/features/solvency-dashboard)** any time you want reassurance.
- Start with **small amounts**, and only scale up once you are comfortable.
- Keep your wallet's recovery phrase **offline and private**.

<Callout title="Transparency is the point">
  Spield exposes its solvency, its code hashes, its pause and admin state, and all its events on-chain so you
  can verify rather than trust. Use that. It is there for you.
</Callout>
